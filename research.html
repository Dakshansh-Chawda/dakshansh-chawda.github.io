PhD Research

Research Focus & Interests

My doctoral research concentrates on machine learning applications in quantum chromodynamics,
with emphasis on neural network-based parton distributions and advanced computational methods
in high-energy physics phenomenology.

Primary Research

Bayesian Neural Networks
for PDFs (NNPDF)

Parton Distribution Functions (PDFs) describe the internal structure of hadrons in terms of quark and gluon constituents. They are fundamental to understanding particle physics at the Large Hadron Collider.

My primary research focuses on the NNPDF methodology, which employs artificial neural networks as unbiased interpolants for PDF parametrization. Key aspects include:

  • Bayesian Methods: Developing robust uncertainty quantification for ML-based PDF fits
  • Neural Network Optimization: Improving training algorithms and exploring modern deep learning techniques
  • Monte Carlo Approach: Generating PDF replicas for faithful statistical representation
  • Validation: Implementing closure tests and rigorous validation methodologies
  • Global Analysis: Combining DIS, Drell-Yan, and collider data for comprehensive PDF determination

This approach provides statistically sound determination of PDF uncertainties—crucial for precision predictions in high-energy physics.

Secondary Research

Flow-Oriented Perturbation
Theory (FOPT)

Flow-Oriented Perturbation Theory represents a novel and elegant diagrammatic approach to perturbative quantum field theory. Rather than traditional Feynman graphs, FOPT uses strongly connected directed graphs (digraphs) in coordinate space.

Key advantages of this approach:

  • Canonical Feynman Rules: Combinatorial and systematic rules for amplitude calculation
  • Simplified Integrals: Reduced complexity in iε dependence and integral evaluation
  • Infrared Factorization: Manifest IR singularity factorization at per-diagram level
  • Geometric Connection: Links to Fourier transforms of flow polytopes
  • Higher-Loop Efficiency: Potential for improved computation of higher-loop amplitudes

The fascinating interplay between perturbative QFT and combinatorial geometry opens new avenues for understanding quantum field theory structure.

Collaboration

NNPDF Collaboration

I am an active participant in the Neural Network Parton Distribution Functions (NNPDF) Collaboration, a global initiative advancing PDF determination through machine learning.

About NNPDF:

  • Open-source collaboration combining machine learning with particle physics
  • Develops computational frameworks for global PDF analysis
  • Widely used by CERN, Tevatron, and LHC collaborations
  • Focuses on uncertainty quantification and methodological innovation
  • Publicly available tools and scientific outputs

The collaboration represents the intersection of modern ML techniques and fundamental physics, driving precision predictions for the LHC era.
