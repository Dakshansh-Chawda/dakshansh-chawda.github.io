About Me

A physicist passionate about understanding the fundamental structure of matter
and applying machine learning to solve challenging problems in theoretical physics

Background & Journey

I am a PhD student in theoretical physics at the University of Milano, Italy. My journey into physics began with curiosity about the fundamental nature of reality—how matter, energy, and forces interact at the deepest levels.

Throughout my studies, I've developed a strong passion for the intersection of theoretical physics and computational methods. The realization that modern machine learning can tackle problems previously considered intractable has been transformative, reshaping how I approach physics research.

Currently, I focus on developing and refining neural network-based approaches for extracting parton distribution functions from experimental data—work that combines rigorous theoretical physics with cutting-edge machine learning techniques.

Research Philosophy

Why Physics?

Physics seeks to understand the universe's fundamental laws. This pursuit is not just intellectually gratifying—it has practical implications for technology, medicine, and society. The tools we develop for fundamental research often find unexpected applications.

Why Machine Learning?

Machine learning represents a paradigm shift in how we approach complex systems. By letting algorithms learn patterns from data, we can tackle problems where traditional analytical approaches struggle. In physics, ML opens doors to new discoveries and more efficient analyses.

Why Theoretical Physics?

Theoretical physics provides the framework for understanding experimental observations. It's where mathematics, physics, and intuition combine to unlock nature's secrets. I'm drawn to the elegance of theoretical descriptions and the challenge of bridging theory and experiment.

Interests Beyond Physics

Science Communication: I believe science should be accessible to everyone. I'm passionate about explaining complex concepts clearly and making physics exciting to broad audiences.

Open Source & Collaborative Science: I strongly support open science initiatives. Sharing code, data, and knowledge accelerates discovery and democratizes access to scientific tools globally.

Teaching & Mentoring: Helping others learn physics and develop as researchers is deeply rewarding. Whether through formal teaching, mentoring, or online content, education is central to my identity as a scientist.

Computational Literacy: In modern physics, computational skills are essential. I'm committed to helping others develop proficiency in programming and computational methods.

Current Focus

As a PhD student, my days involve:

  • Developing Bayesian approaches to neural network training for PDF fits
  • Implementing and testing new machine learning algorithms
  • Analyzing experimental data from high-energy colliders
  • Writing papers communicating research findings
  • Collaborating with physicists worldwide through the NNPDF Collaboration
  • Supporting undergraduate students through teaching assistance
  • Continuously learning new computational techniques and physics concepts

Looking Forward

My vision for the future combines several threads:

  • Research: Continue advancing machine learning applications in particle physics, potentially exploring new theoretical frameworks
  • Impact: Develop tools and methods that the physics community can use for decades
  • Education: Mentor next-generation physicists and contribute to physics education
  • Communication: Make theoretical physics accessible and inspiring to broad audiences
  • Collaboration: Work with diverse teams combining physicists, machine learning experts, and computer scientists

I'm excited about the future of physics—a field increasingly shaped by computational power and artificial intelligence. The questions are fundamental, the challenges are real, and the potential for discovery is immense.
